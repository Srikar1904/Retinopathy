Understanding Diabetic Retinopathy

The eye can reveal what diabetes hides.

Diabetic Retinopathy affects the blood vessels of the retina. RetinaCare AI is designed to support early screening by analysing retinal fundus images and classifying the severity of diabetic retinopathy across five stages.

Why it matters

Understanding the condition is the first step.

Diabetic retinopathy can progress gradually, making regular retinal screening important for identifying changes before significant vision problems occur.

01 / RETINA
◉

Changes in retinal vessels

Diabetes can damage small blood vessels supplying the retina, leading to retinal changes that can be observed in fundus images.

02 / SCREENING
⌁

Early screening matters

Screening can help identify retinal abnormalities and support timely referral for appropriate clinical evaluation.

The human eye

Where the signal begins.

The retina is a light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. Retinal fundus photographs provide the visual information analysed by the RetinaCare AI model.

Retina — light-sensitive tissue
Macula — central vision region
Optic disc — optic nerve exit
Retinal vessels — visible vascular structures

Why RetinaCare AI

Turning retinal images into meaningful screening insights.

RetinaCare AI combines a ResNet-50 deep learning model, automated image preprocessing and Grad-CAM explainability to create an AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening workflow.

Important: RetinaCare AI is a research and screening prototype. Its predictions are not a medical diagnosis and should not replace evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.
